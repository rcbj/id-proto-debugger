Gordian Envelope — a credential you elide rather than re-sign

Gordian Envelope (IETF draft-mcnally-envelope-12, over dCBOR, draft-18) is a Merkle tree of digests: any claim can be elided — replaced by its digest — and every digest above it, so the issuer's signature over the root, stays the same. A holder presents by eliding; nothing is re-signed. Credentials are signed wrapped, so every claim is under the signature. Text form: ur:envelope/…. Elements can also be encrypted (to a key, to recipients, or under a password), compressed, or their key split into SSKR shares; large envelopes travel as multi-part URs. Held byte for byte to Blockchain Commons' Rust crates' own test vectors. Nothing on this page is stored: seeds, keys and passwords live only in this page load.

Read, Verify and Present

ur:signing-public-key/…, ur:crypto-pubkeys/… (Schnorr, ECDSA, Ed25519, ML-DSA-44/65/87 or an SSH key), an OpenSSH public key line (ssh-ed25519 AAAA…, ecdsa-sha2-nistp256 AAAA…), or hex: 32 bytes for a Schnorr x-only key, 33 for a compressed ECDSA key.

  Issue a Credential
  

    
Load bc-envelope's Certificate of Completion example (signed with the Rust tests' public "Alice" seed).

    

      
A PrivateKeyBase seed (hex). The signing key is HKDF-SHA-256 of it with the salt "signing", as bc-components derives it; an SSH Ed25519 key is HKDF-SHA-256 with the salt "ssh-ed25519-0", as bc-components' HKDFRng gives it. bc-components derives no ML-DSA key from a seed: for ML-DSA this page uses HKDF-SHA-256(seed, "signing") as FIPS 204's 32-byte key generation seed, which is this page's choice.

        
          
          

      
The issuer's signing public key (ur:signing-public-key/…, or the OpenSSH form's tagged text for an SSH key), derived from the seed. Give it to verifiers.

        

      
The credential's subject: a 32-byte ARID (hex). Empty: a random one.

        

      
One "predicate: value" per line. 'isA' in single quotes is a known value; "date 2020-01-01" is a Date; a whole number is a number; anything else is text.

        

      
Give every claim its own salt, so an elided claim cannot be guessed from its digest.

        

      
An optional 'note' assertion added after signing, so it is outside the signature.

  Encrypt and Decrypt
  

    
An ENCRYPTED element (tag 40002) is ChaCha20-Poly1305 over the element's tagged CBOR, authenticated with its digest, so the tree's digests — and a signature over them — do not change. The content key can be given directly, sealed to recipients ('hasRecipient'), or locked with a password ('hasSecret'). As bc-envelope 0.43 and bc-envelope-cli 0.35 do it. Nothing here is stored.

    

      
The envelope to encrypt or decrypt: ur:envelope/…, its parts, or the hex of its CBOR.

        

      
Wrap the envelope before encrypting, so its assertions are encrypted with its subject (bc-envelope's encrypt()). Unticked, only the subject is encrypted and the assertions stay readable (encrypt_subject(), what bc-envelope-cli's encrypt does).

        

      
The 32-byte symmetric key: ur:crypto-key/… or 64 hex digits. Empty when encrypting: a new random one (shown here afterwards). Kept only in this field, for this page load.

        
          

      
Public keys to seal the content key to, one ur:crypto-pubkeys/… per line (an X25519 or ML-KEM-512/768/1024 encapsulation key). Each becomes a 'hasRecipient': SealedMessage assertion.

        

      
A password to lock the content key with ('hasSecret': EncryptedKey). Also what Decrypt tries. Never stored.

        
          

      
For Decrypt as a recipient: ur:crypto-prvkeys/…, ur:crypto-prvkey-base/…, ur:seed/…, or a seed in hex (its X25519 key is HKDF-SHA-256(seed, "agreement"), as bc-components derives it). Kept only in this field.

        

      

          

      
 

      
The encrypted or decrypted envelope, as ur:envelope/…. Read It shows it in the first pane.

        
          

      

      
Make a recipient's keys: from a seed (hex; empty for a random one) with X25519, as bc-components' PrivateKeyBase does, or a random ML-KEM pair. The private keys go into the Private keys field and the public keys into Recipients; neither is stored.

        
          
          

      
The recipient's ur:crypto-pubkeys/…: give it to whoever encrypts to them. Make Keys also adds it to Recipients.

        

    

  






  Compress and Decompress
  

    
A COMPRESSED element (tag 40003) is [CRC-32, size, raw DEFLATE, digest]: its digest is the uncompressed element's, so nothing above it changes. The DEFLATE bytes need not be miniz's (bc-components') — the digest does not cover them.

    

      
The envelope to compress or decompress: ur:envelope/…, its parts, or the hex of its CBOR.

        

      
Compress (or decompress) only the subject, leaving the assertions — a signature, say — outside, as bc-envelope's compress_subject() does.

        

      

          

      
 

      
The compressed or decompressed envelope, as ur:envelope/…. Its digest is unchanged.

        
          

    

  






  SSKR: Split and Join
  

    
Sharded Secret Key Reconstruction (BCR-2020-011): the envelope is wrapped and encrypted, and its content key split by Shamir's scheme into groups of shares. Each share envelope is the encrypted envelope with one 'sskrShare'; any shares meeting the thresholds rejoin it. As bc-envelope-cli's sskr split and sskr join.

    

      
The envelope to split.

        

      
One "m-of-n" per group, separated by commas (2-of-3, 3-of-5): n shares in the group, any m of which recover the group's secret. At most 16 groups of at most 16 shares.

        

      
How many groups must meet their own threshold (1 to the number of groups).

        

      

      
Share envelopes, one ur:envelope/… per line. Split writes every share here; for Join, paste the ones you have, in any order.

        

      

      
 

      
The envelope the shares rebuild, as ur:envelope/….

        
          

    

  






  Multi-part UR
  

    
An envelope too large for one QR code is sent as ur:envelope/<seq>-<count>/… parts (BCR-2020-005, BCR-2024-001): the first count are its fragments and every later one a fountain part, the XOR of a pseudo-random set of fragments, so any large enough set of parts, in any order, rebuilds it. As the ur 0.4.1 crate (bc-ur 0.19) writes them.

    

      
The envelope to split into parts: ur:envelope/… or the hex of its CBOR.

        

      
The most CBOR bytes in one part's fragment; the fragments are then made equal in length.

        

      
How many mixed parts to write after the fragments themselves.

        

      

      
One part per line. Make Parts writes them here; for Join Parts, paste any parts of one envelope, in any order.

        

      

      
 

      
The envelope the parts rebuild, as a single-part ur:envelope/….
